# Data Interpretation Reference

Use this reference when combining Google Ads with GA4, GSC, GTM, Merchant Center or PageSpeed evidence.

## General rule

Do not compare metrics from different systems as if they were generated by the same measurement model.

Always consider:

- attribution logic
- consent
- freshness
- reporting lag
- conversion lag
- campaign changes
- incomplete periods

## Google Ads vs GA4

Ads clicks and GA4 sessions are not expected to match exactly.

Reasons include:

- consent rejection
- attribution differences
- sessionization
- redirects
- tracking blockers
- missing UTMs or linker issues
- conversion windows

Do not diagnose tracking as broken solely because Ads clicks are higher than GA4 sessions.

Use cross-reference tools and broader evidence.

## GDPR / consent

For consent-heavy markets, GA4 may observe fewer sessions/events than Ads observes clicks.

Consent Mode can reduce the discrepancy but does not make the two systems identical.

When diagnosing tracking:

1. inspect Ads vs GA4 discrepancy
2. consider consent
3. check source-wide behavior
4. inspect tracking events
5. inspect GTM/code only when needed

## Conversion lag

Recent periods can understate conversion performance.

Before concluding that CPA or ROAS deteriorated:

- consider the account's sales cycle
- compare mature cohorts where possible
- avoid overreacting to the most recent incomplete days

## Search Console

GSC is not real time and commonly lags other Google marketing data.

Avoid comparing GSC through yesterday against Ads/GA4 including today as if the windows were equally complete.

Use GSC mainly for organic query/page context, not as a substitute for Ads performance data.

## Merchant Center

Product/feed status can lag recent feed updates.

For Shopping or ecommerce PMax delivery issues:

- inspect feed health first
- surface major disapprovals/account issues
- allow for propagation lag after a fresh correction

Do not promise feed edits through AdLoop Merchant tools if they are read-only.

## PMax transparency

Respect Google Ads API limitations.

Do not claim:

- a complete reliable Search vs YouTube vs Display vs Discover split when unavailable
- per-asset BEST/GOOD/LOW ratings if the API does not expose them
- explicit audience targeting behavior where PMax uses automated targeting

Use the signals that actually exist, such as campaign metrics, asset-group strength, serving status and detailed asset combinations.

## Recommendations from Google

Treat platform recommendations as inputs, not truth.

Evaluate them against:

- business goals
- conversion integrity
- marginal economics
- budget constraints
- current campaign structure

Do not automatically apply recommendation-engine suggestions.

## PageSpeed

Lab and field data serve different purposes.

- field data reflects real-user history
- lab data is controlled simulation

When both exist and conflict, treat field data as the stronger real-world performance signal.

Missing field data can mean insufficient traffic rather than good performance.

## Quality Score

Quality Score is diagnostic, not the final business objective.

Prioritize high-spend, high-opportunity keywords and the actual subcomponent causing weakness.

Avoid optimizing low-volume Quality Score for its own sake when CPA/ROAS and business outcomes are healthy.

## Auction/competition analysis

Competitive metrics can explain pressure, but they do not prove competitor budgets, intent or strategy.

Do not present inferred competitor behavior as fact.

Use competition findings together with CPC, impression share, profitability and conversion data.

## Forecasts

Keyword Planner and budget forecasts are estimates.

Present them as scenarios, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Period comparisons

Preferred comparisons include:

- last 7 days vs previous 7 days
- last 30 days vs previous 30 days
- month-to-date vs equivalent prior-month days

Avoid comparing complete and incomplete windows without explicitly noting the limitation.
